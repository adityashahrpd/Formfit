Nutrition basics

Eat to support training.

There is no single “perfect diet.” Especially for teenagers, regular balanced meals and adequate nutrition matter more than aggressive dieting.

Build a balanced plate

At most meals, combine several food groups rather than relying on one food or supplement.

01
Protein foodsMilk/curd, eggs, dal, beans, paneer, soy, fish or meat if you eat them.
02
CarbohydratesRice, roti, oats, potatoes, fruits and other grains provide training fuel.
03
Plants + micronutrientsInclude vegetables and fruit across the day in a variety of colours.
04
Healthy fatsNuts, seeds, dairy, avocado and suitable cooking oils can fit into balanced meals.

Before & after training

Choose foods you tolerate well. You do not need complicated supplements to begin strength training.

↗
BeforeA normal meal or snack with carbohydrates and some protein can work.
↘
AfterHave a normal balanced meal/snack containing protein and carbohydrate.
💧
HydrationDrink regularly through the day and more around exercise/heat. Avoid forcing excessive water.
😴
RecoverySleep and rest are part of training — not something to “earn.”
Equipment guide

Know what you're using.

Simple rules for common gym equipment can make training safer and more effective.

🏋️

Dumbbells

Keep a controlled path and choose a weight you can move without losing form.

🪑

Bench

Check the adjustment and stability before loading it. Keep your body supported.

🧵

Cables

Set the pin securely and check the attachment before each set.

🔩

Machines

Adjust seat and pads so joints line up with the machine's intended movement.

Smart training

Progress slowly. Move well.

If you're new to a gym, ask a qualified trainer to demonstrate unfamiliar equipment. Stop an exercise if you get sharp pain, dizziness, chest pain or feel unwell.
